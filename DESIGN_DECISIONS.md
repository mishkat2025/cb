# Design decisions

Every choice the proposal, the dataset paper or the data leave open, with one line of reasoning. Each is configurable: Phase 1 keys live in `configs/data.yaml` and `configs/exercises/EXX.yaml`; keys for later phases are the intended YAML names. "proposed (measured)" = my choice, backed by a Phase 1 measurement, still open to change.

**Status:**
- **decided:** approved by the supervisor, or fixed by the spec
- **proposed:** my recommendation, open to change
- **TBD:** to be set in the phase named

## Pose extraction and preprocessing

| # | Decision | Reasoning | Config key | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | MediaPipe 1.1.1 Tasks `PoseLandmarker` (VIDEO mode), full model by default (heavy optional). Save 2D (x, y, z, visibility, presence) and world landmarks, one compressed `.npz` per video (a bundle of `.npy` arrays) plus `data/index.csv`. Add `HandLandmarker` for E06 later; extraction refuses E06 until the hand model is approved. | Legacy `solutions` no longer exists in 1.1.1. Full model ≈ 24 fps per process with 12 processes (~285 fps total). | `pose.model`, `pose.hands_for` | decided |
| P1b | Frames are decoded with PyAV: real presentation timestamps (VIDEO-mode timestamps and the stored `t`), the rotation flag applied (`np.rot90(frame, rotation // 90)`, verified against OpenCV's auto-rotation: mean abs pixel difference 0.4 vs 50 for the wrong direction). | D9. | – | decided |
| P2 | Downscale frames to 960 px on the long side before pose estimation. | Phase 1 check on 12 videos (all views, FL/LL/O/HJ/LJ/LR, expert and non-expert; `outputs/phase1/pose_settings_check.csv`): detection 99.96% at both 960 px and full resolution; median landmark difference 960 vs full = 0.8% of torso length (pose-noise level); 960 px is ~25% faster. | `pose.max_side: 960` | proposed (measured) |
| P3 | No square padding. | Same check: shoulder-width/torso ratio (which a horizontal squash would change) agrees within 0.01 between padded and unpadded frames on 11 of 12 videos (the 352×640 LR video: 0.652 vs 0.627); landmark differences ≤ 1% of torso length. The MediaPipe "NORM_RECT" warning causes no measurable distortion. | `pose.pad_square: false` | proposed (measured) |
| P4 | Joint set **coco17** (nose, eyes, ears, shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles) for the network; the cache keeps all 33 landmarks. ST-GCN graph = the standard COCO layout (mmaction2/pyskl `coco`, 16 edges, centre = nose), in `cvdc/data/skeleton.py`. | Keeps every joint the E01/E02 angles need; drops the noisy face, finger and foot points; a standard ST-GCN layout. `mp33` stays selectable. | `preprocess.joint_set` | proposed |
| P5 | Uniform 30 fps grid from real timestamps (also brings the 20 fps PT demos to 30 fps); root = mid-hip; scale = median 2D torso length (3D: median 3D torso length); Savitzky-Golay 7 frames, order 2; fixed T_rep = 64 and T_video = 300 for the network (`cvdc/data/pack.py`), full-length sequences kept for DTW. Frames without a pose are always interpolated; **low-visibility joints (< 0.5) are interpolated only for runs ≤ 0.5 s**, longer runs keep MediaPipe's estimate (flagged by the visibility channel). | Hips (both ≥ 0.5 visibility) are visible in 99.6/94.3/99.8% of frames on average for E01 expert F/L/R videos (E02: 99.9/95.5/99.9%; non-experts ≥ 99.6%), so mid-hip root. Rep length median 64 frames at 30 fps (p5 37, p95 89), so T_rep = 64. Visibility check: in AL videos the left elbow/wrist are visible in only 4–20% of frames and in AR videos the right elbow/wrist in 40–71% (self-occlusion by the trunk, not dropouts). Interpolating across those runs froze the moving arm (25% of upper-body joint-frames were replaced in the first test). | `preprocess.*` | proposed (measured) |
| P6 | Rep segmentation (`cvdc/data/reps.py`): 0.3 s running median, then peaks with prominence and ≥ 1 s spacing; a rep runs from where the signal leaves its rest level (10% of the amplitude above the neighbouring minimum) to where it returns, ±0.1 s; reps with amplitude below the minimum on either side (e.g. a video ending with the arm up) or lasting outside 0.8–15 s are dropped. Moving arm = the side with the larger p5–p95 range. **E01:** 3D shoulder elevation (trunk line vs upper arm), prominence and minimum amplitude 30°. **E02:** PC1 of the wrist path relative to the shoulder in the trunk frame (3D), prominence 0.25 torso lengths. **This is not a joint angle**: approved by the supervisor for segmentation only (Module III uses joint angles, M7a). | Chosen by cross-view rep-count agreement in the 129 expert F/L/R groups per exercise (same performance, so the views must agree): E01 3D elevation 69% within ±1 rep (2D wrist-height variant 69%, PC1 66–68%); E02 PC1 42%, best angle 36% (2D height), 3D elevation 6–12%, signed frontal-plane angle 14–27%. E02 is a cross-body adduction with the arm low (elevation ~10–45°; the PT demo `Aduction.mp4` shows the same movement), whose direction changes with the view. PC1 is oriented so that moving toward the midline is positive: in the 45 audio-synced E02 groups, rep peaks then coincide across views (±0.4 s after alignment) in 70/62/48% (F–L/F–R/L–R) vs 32/38/36% with a 'longer tail = movement' orientation (E01 3D elevation: 68/71/56%). The median removes 1–3 frame 3D glitches that split reps. | `reps.*`, `exercises/EXX.yaml` | decided (E02 signal: segmentation only) |
| P6b | **Synced groups get identical rep boundaries in every aligned view** (supervisor, 2026-10-17): reps are segmented on the aligned view with the best signal quality (share of frames with both shoulders, both hips and the moving elbow and wrist visible ≥ 0.5; ties F > R > L) and transferred to the other views with the group's offsets; the group's moving arm is the best view's. A rep some aligned view did not record (the phones start/stop up to ~1 s apart; tolerance 0.1 s) is dropped from every view (`common`), so rep sets are identical; `n_reps_set` keeps the best view's full count as the set's rep count (for PF4 / the EAAQ). Reps share `rep_uid` across views. Not-synced, unknown and single-view videos keep per-view segmentation. | Cross-view agreement in synced groups: 100% by construction (before: exact 32% / 15%, ±1 69% / 42% for E01 / E02); transferred boundaries coincide on the aligned clock within 0.96 frames. Best view: F in 116/127 (E01) and 115/127 (E02) groups. Rep counts changed in 86 (E01) and 108 (E02) of 127 groups (median 1 and 2 reps over the group's views, p90 5.4 and 9.4); 96 groups per exercise lost an edge rep that some view did not record (360 and 356 rep instances summed over views for E01 and E02). Training reps 7,827 → 7,095. Against PF4 (E01 experts) the set count does better than per-view counts: Spearman 0.36 vs 0.24; PF4 = 1 → ≥ 10 reps in 98% vs 88%. | `reps.view_preference`, `reps.transfer_tol_s`, `reps.transfer_keep: common \| covered` | decided (measured) |
| P7 | Videos with a detected pose in < 50% of frames get no preprocessed file and are skipped by reps, packs and sync (listed in `outputs/phase1/preprocess_summary.csv`). Joints never observed in a kept video are placed at the root with visibility 0. | 8 videos, all left-view takes in LL or HJ (0–40% detection; the rest of their groups is kept). | `preprocess.min_detection_rate: 0.5` | proposed |
| P8 | The moving arm is detected per group (best view), not assumed. Left-arm performances are **mirrored and assessed normally**; **no `arm_side` flag** in the Module III output. | ~14% of expert performances use the left arm (all three views agree, front views included, so not a label swap). Supervisor rule: flag only if expert scores are lower for left-arm performances. Test (`scripts/phase1/left_arm_scores.py`, unit = performance, views averaged; one-sided Mann-Whitney, Holm over 2 exercises × total/PF/CF, rule fixed before running): no score is significantly lower (Holm p ≥ 0.08). Borderline: CF is lower in both exercises (E01 0.74 vs 0.85, E02 0.81 vs 0.86; uncorrected p 0.028 / 0.014; within-subject n.s.), from only 15 and 22 left-arm performances of 10 subjects each. Total and PF show no difference. | `assess.mirror_left_arm: true`, `assess.flag_arm_side: false` | decided (measured) |

## Views and synchronization

| # | Decision | Reasoning | Config key | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Classify each group as synced / not-synced / unknown using **audio, then container creation_time, then motion and rep timing**. Store lag, confidence and method in `outputs/sync_report.csv`. | Supervisor change 2; the dataset can't be changed. | `sync.order` | decided |
| S2 | Audio: GCC-PHAT on 8 kHz mono, 100–3800 Hz, ±3 s window. Group-level proof = lag additivity, \|lag_FL + lag_LR − lag_FR\| ≤ 20 ms. | Peak height is weak (quiet room), but peak location matched motion on all 23 motion-confirmed pairs; additivity rejects chance peaks. Passes for 36% of expert groups. | `sync.audio.*` | decided |
| S3 | Creation time: \|ΔC\| ≤ 3 s on all pairs → synced (medium confidence). Recording windows that can't overlap (\|ΔC\| > dur_a + dur_b) → not-synced (high). | No control pair was ever ≤ 3 s (minimum 4 s); ~99% of expert pairs are. E01 non-expert pairs are 11–97 s apart. | `sync.meta.max_dc_s: 3` | decided |
| S4 | Refine lags with motion inside ±0.3 s of the device-corrected audio lag (±5 s when there is no usable audio lag). Pass if the rep-timing residual (offset and linear drift removed) is under 42 ms with ≥ 4 matched crossings. Signals: 2D wrist height, 3D shoulder elevation, wrist PC1 (best cross-correlation). | Audio and video clocks differ per phone; motion gives the frame-level lag. Phase 1: 217 of 818 pairs confirmed. Controls (same subject, different variation, 2006 pairs, 1471 testable) searched over ±5 s: 11 falsely confirmed (0.7%); control residual p1 = 47 ms, median 183 ms. In three-view groups the confirmed lags are consistent: least-squares offset residual median 3.2 ms. A rising/falling-crossing variant of the residual was tried and did not help, so it was dropped. | `sync.motion.*` | proposed (measured) |
| S5 | Per-device audio-to-video offsets, re-estimated on motion-confirmed simultaneous pairs (\|ΔC\| ≤ 3 s): F→L +93 ms (n = 102, SD 58), F→R +57 ms (n = 77, SD 48), L→R −44 ms (n = 35). Step 0 had +126 / +36 ms from 8 and 11 pairs. Audio lags are converted to each video's own timeline with the audio start time and the first frame's time. | A per-phone offset exists but varies by ~50 ms, so audio-only lags are good to about 1–2 frames. | `sync.device_offsets_ms` (priors) | proposed (measured) |
| S6 | Number of views is configurable (3 now: F/L/R; the proposal's 4 later). | The dataset has 3 views, not 4. | `views.names` | decided |
| S7 | Per-view offsets = weighted least squares over the group's pair lags: motion-confirmed lags weight 1, device-corrected audio lags weight 0.04 (≈ (10 ms / 50 ms)², their SD ratio). | Motion lags dominate where they exist; audio lags fill the remaining views (supervisor answer 3). A test checks that a 50 ms-off audio lag moves the offsets by < 2 ms. | `sync.audio_lag_weight` | proposed |

## CVDC (contrastive pretraining)

| # | Decision | Reasoning | Config key | Status |
